Tales From Below · Tale II

The Pious

A revered religious leader builds a public life around salvation while privately weaponizing fear, shame, and obedience.

Approx. Time
October 13, at the ninth bell after the cathedral funeral begins.
Holiday
The Feast of the Scapegoat.
Reader Note
The holiday sharpens the accusation: one celebrated man is asked who benefited when others carried his sins.
First Descent

The bishop died beneath a ceiling painted with angels. His followers filled the cathedral before dawn, weeping into silk handkerchiefs and calling his name as if sound could pull a soul upward.

He smiled when the light came for him. He had rehearsed that smile for forty years: calm, humble, certain. It lasted until the light changed color.

There was no choir waiting below. Only a corridor made from church doors, each one carved with the face of someone he had corrected, shamed, exiled, silenced, or used. Their mouths moved without sound. Their eyes followed him.

At the end of the corridor, a small clerk dipped a pen into a bowl of flame and said, "State your name as you gave it to the poor, not as you printed it on the building."
