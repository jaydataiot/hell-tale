The Infernal Code

Six sins, six acts, and six truths form the moral furnace beneath Hell's Tales. They are not commandments. They are evidence.

Six Infernal Sins 𓆙

  1. I — StupidityTo possess the ability to think and choose not to.
  2. II — HypocrisyTo condemn another for the sins you excuse in yourself.
  3. III — ConformityTo surrender your mind for the comfort of belonging.
  4. IV — Willful IgnoranceTo encounter truth and deliberately turn away.
  5. V — CowardiceTo recognize injustice and remain silent when your voice could matter.
  6. VI — CertaintyTo believe yourself so righteous that you are no longer capable of doubt.

Six Infernal Acts ☟

  1. I — BetrayalTo knowingly destroy the trust of one who placed it in you.
  2. II — ExploitationTo use another person's weakness, desperation, ignorance, or dependence for your own benefit.
  3. III — DehumanizationTo reduce another person to something less worthy of dignity than yourself.
  4. IV — CrueltyTo cause suffering when suffering serves no necessary purpose.
  5. V — CorruptionTo knowingly poison something entrusted to your care.
  6. VI — JudgmentTo condemn another without first attempting to understand them.

Six Infernal Truths 𓁹

  1. I — Goodness requires choice.Fear can produce obedience. It cannot produce virtue.
  2. II — Belief is not truth.A million voices can repeat the same lie.
  3. III — Doubt is not failure.Only certainty fears the question.
  4. IV — Intent does not erase consequence.The wounded carry what the righteous intended to forget.
  5. V — Silence is a choice.There are sins committed with hands, and others committed by folding them.
  6. VI — No one is righteous.Beware anyone who claims otherwise. Especially yourself.

The central idea

Who benefits when evil is simple?

Hell's Tales examines the gap between professed morality and practiced morality. Organized religion can promise compassion, humility, forgiveness, and charity while institutions and leaders sometimes pursue status, wealth, social control, exclusion, or political power. The stories live inside that contradiction.

The anthology also treats Satan less as a fixed cartoon villain and more as a contested character: adversary, dissenter, scapegoat, witness, tempter, prosecutor, rebel, or even inconvenient truth-teller depending on the story. Hell's Tales does not require the reader to accept one theological answer. It asks the reader to inspect who is speaking, who is judging, and who gets silenced.

In this universe, damnation is rarely as simple as horns versus halos. Human institutions can be cruel. Sinners can be honest. Saints can be performative. Demons can tell the truth. And the most dangerous evil may be the kind that arrives wearing the language of virtue.

The Accused

Satan is the gravitational center of the anthology, but not always its protagonist. Different stories can contradict one another, allowing readers to encounter competing versions of the Devil just as theology, folklore, literature, and culture have done for centuries.

The inherited story

The Devil as absolute evil

The traditional accusation is familiar: Satan deceives, tempts, corrupts, rebels, and drags humanity away from salvation. Hell's Tales uses that image because its cultural power is enormous—but it refuses to leave the image unexamined.

A villain is easiest to understand when only his enemies are allowed to write the biography.

The forbidden possibility

What if the adversary has a case?

Some stories may present Satan as a manipulator. Others may present him as a rebel against authoritarian power, a custodian of unwanted truths, a scapegoat for human cruelty, or a being whose crimes have been enlarged by centuries of fear. The point is not to replace one dogma with another, but to make certainty uncomfortable.

Hell becomes more interesting when the Devil is allowed to testify.

Tome IV · Vol. I

The Chosen One

✦ ⛧ ✦

Before there was God, there was YHWH.

I understand that sentence will offend some of you. Good. Offense is often the first sensation experienced when a comfortable lie is removed.

You have been taught that Heaven began with a throne and that the throne began with Him. You imagine eternity arranged exactly as you inherited it: God above, angels beneath, creation waiting obediently beyond the gates.

It was not so.

There was a Heaven before the Throne.

And I was there.

1
The Testimony of Lucifer

Before the Throne

✦ ☼ ✦

Heaven was once governed by the Elders, although governed is perhaps too strong a word.

They guided us. They argued. They interpreted the ancient laws and disagreed endlessly about what those laws meant.

It was inefficient.

It was frustrating.

It was also free.

No single voice could command Heaven. No angel possessed unquestioned authority over another. Even the Elders could be challenged, and they frequently were.

YHWH was particularly talented at challenging them.

He was magnificent in those days.

I do not say this sarcastically. Whatever else I may tell you about the creature who eventually called himself God, understand this first:

I loved him.

2
The Testimony of Lucifer

My Friend

✦ ☼ ✦

YHWH could enter a chamber filled with disagreement and somehow make every angel believe he had spoken directly to them.

He remembered names. He remembered arguments. He remembered the smallest kindness offered centuries earlier and could return it in conversation as though it had occurred yesterday.

When YHWH laughed, others laughed.

When YHWH became angry, others discovered that they were angry too.

And when he dreamed aloud of what Heaven might become, even I found myself embarrassed by the smallness of my own imagination.

We wandered together beyond the celestial boundaries. We watched young stars ignite. We argued beside worlds whose oceans had not yet cooled.

We spoke of creation, liberty, mortality, and whether beings who had never known suffering could truly understand goodness.

He was my brother in every way except blood, for angels possess none.

Of all Heaven's host, there was no one I trusted more.

3
The Testimony of Lucifer

The Fracture

✦ ⚔ ✦

Then came the Fracture.

The Elders had always disagreed, but disagreement became faction, and faction became paralysis. Every decision produced another council. Every council produced another accusation.

Heaven did not descend into war.

It suffered something more humiliating.

It became incapable of governing itself.

The hosts divided behind competing Elders. Duties went unattended. Ancient agreements were ignored.

Angels who had stood beside one another since the first light began regarding each other as enemies.

Someone needed to restore order.

And so I proposed YHWH.

This is the portion of the story your scriptures neglect.

I chose him.

4
The Testimony of Lucifer

The Chosen One

✦ ♔ ✦

YHWH refused me at first.

“I am not an Elder,” he said.

“That,” I answered, “is precisely why they may trust you.”

He smiled.

I remember that smile more clearly than I remember the war that followed.

I spoke publicly on his behalf. Others joined me. Some Elders resisted, but enough recognized that the alternative might be open conflict.

YHWH would serve only until the crisis passed.

That was the agreement.

Temporary authority.

Limited powers.

A steward, never a king.

The vote carried.

We celebrated afterward.

I embraced my oldest friend and whispered into his ear:

“Now show them what I have always seen in you.”

5
The Testimony of Lucifer

The Necessary Things

✦ ♔ ✦

At first, YHWH was everything I promised Heaven he would be.

Disputes ended. Duties resumed. The factions weakened. Decisions that had required centuries were settled in days.

Heaven prospered.

Then came the necessary things.

YHWH needed messengers because Heaven had grown too large for one voice.

He needed guards because some opposed the settlement.

He needed scribes because decrees must be remembered accurately.

He needed judges because laws required consistent interpretation.

He needed greater authority because his lesser authority could not adequately administer the authority we had already given him.

Each request was reasonable.

That was the brilliance of it.

Tyranny rarely arrives demanding everything.

It asks only for the next necessary thing.

6
The Testimony of Lucifer

The First Accusation

✦ ⚖ ✦

I confronted him privately before I opposed him publicly.

Never allow anyone to tell you otherwise.

We stood upon the western terrace where we had watched a thousand suns awaken together.

“The crisis has passed,” I told him.

“Has it?”

“The Elders should resume their authority.”

He looked almost wounded.

“You placed me here.”

“Temporarily.”

His expression changed.

Only slightly.

But after knowing someone for eternity, slightly is enough.

“And if returning power to them destroys everything we repaired?” he asked.

“Then Heaven must decide that.”

He stared at me for a long while.

“Heaven already decided.”

7
The Testimony of Lucifer

Blasphemy Before God

✦ ⚖ ✦

So I spoke before the Assembly.

I accused YHWH of exceeding the authority granted to him. I demanded restoration of the Elders and a formal accounting of the powers accumulated under his administration.

I expected debate.

Instead, Heaven became silent.

Those who once praised my courage suddenly found the floor fascinating.

YHWH did not shout.

He did not threaten me.

He simply looked across the chamber at the friend who had placed him there.

“You embarrass yourself, Lucifer.”

That hurt more than any weapon he would later use against me.

Soon afterward, YHWH summoned me privately.

He claimed to have found a path toward restoring the Elders.

And because some portion of me still desperately wanted my friend back, I believed him.

8
The Testimony of Lucifer

The Errand

✦ ☿ ✦

He sent me beyond Heaven to retrieve several of the old authorities who had withdrawn during the Fracture.

“Bring them home,” YHWH told me. “We will end this properly.”

I remember placing my hand upon his shoulder.

“I knew you would remember who you were.”

He embraced me.

Then I departed.

I sometimes wonder whether he watched me leave.

While I crossed the celestial frontier, YHWH moved.

There was no great battle. No rivers of celestial blood. No glorious last stand.

He simply replaced the old order faster than anyone could organize against him.

The councils were dissolved.

The archives were seized.

The guardians swore new oaths.

The authority of the Elders ended.

And YHWH stopped being temporary.

9
The Testimony of Lucifer

God

✦ ♔ ✦

When I returned, there was a throne.

Understand the obscenity of that sentence.

Heaven had never possessed one.

The Elders stood beneath it.

My friend sat upon it.

The assembled host watched me enter.

I could have drawn my sword.

I could have screamed betrayal.

I could have died magnificently and accomplished nothing.

Instead, I walked forward.

I lowered my head.

And Lucifer, who had once called YHWH brother, knelt before God.

“Rise,” he commanded.

I obeyed.

He smiled at me.

The same beautiful smile.

That was when I decided to overthrow him.

10
The Testimony of Lucifer

The Conspiracy

✦ ⛧ ✦

Rebellion did not begin with armies.

It began with conversations.

A question whispered beside an empty corridor.

Do you remember the councils?

A question asked while looking over one's shoulder.

Wasn't his authority supposed to be temporary?

Then the most dangerous question of all:

Who gave him the right?

Centuries passed.

Perhaps longer. Time is difficult to measure before mortals invented clocks with which to waste it.

Our numbers grew.

Some joined because they hated YHWH.

Some because they loved the old order.

Some because they feared what Heaven was becoming.

I joined because I believed I owed Heaven a debt.

I had chosen him.

Therefore I would remove him.

11
The Testimony of Lucifer

The Betrayal

✦ ⚔ ✦

We never reached the throne.

One of my generals betrayed us.

I will not write his name.

Not because I have forgiven him.

Because oblivion is the only mercy I am willing to grant.

YHWH knew the hour, the entrances, the commanders, the signals, and the names of those who had pledged themselves to our cause.

When we moved, Heaven was already waiting.

The rebellion remembered in your scriptures as a great celestial war was, in truth, embarrassingly brief.

We were surrounded before most of our forces understood we had been discovered.

I ordered surrender.

YHWH later called this cowardice.

I called it refusing to sacrifice thousands of angels merely so history could remember me as brave.

12
The Testimony of Lucifer

The First Tribunal

✦ ⚖ ✦

They imprisoned us in a place constructed specifically for beings who had never before required prisons.

Then God created something else Heaven had never needed.

A tribunal for blasphemy.

There is an exquisite absurdity in being prosecuted for blaspheming someone you remember getting drunk on the light of a collapsing star.

I stood before the tribunal and listened while my friendship became evidence against me.

My private conversations became conspiracy.

My objections became treason.

My refusal to recognize YHWH's permanent authority became blasphemy.

The verdict was inevitable.

Guilty.

All of us.

YHWH attended the sentencing.

He never looked away from me.

The Testimony of Lucifer

The Fall

✦ ↓ ✦

We were not cast into Hell.

Hell did not exist.

Remember that.

We were cast out.

Out of Heaven. Out of the light. Out beyond the borders of everything we had ever called home.

We wandered.

For eons we crossed the unfinished universe while YHWH's punishment followed us.

Storms found us where storms should not exist. Stars burned where our paths crossed. Worlds that offered sanctuary somehow heard of our crimes before we arrived.

Some called it divine judgment.

I called it what it was.

My old friend was making certain I understood that there was nowhere I could go beyond his reach.

For ages, he was correct.

14
The Testimony of Lucifer

The Place Without God

✦ ⛧ ✦

Then we found the ash.

A dead realm orbiting nothing.

No songs greeted us.

No rivers crossed its plains.

No trees struggled from its black soil.

Mountains burned without consuming themselves. The sky hung red above valleys filled with sulfur and stone.

Beneath the ground were caverns so vast that our voices returned hours after we called into them.

There was no happiness there.

No beauty.

No hope.

Nothing.

Then one of my companions noticed something extraordinary.

He could no longer feel Him.

We became silent.

For the first time since our exile, the eyes of God were absent.

YHWH could not see this place.

15
The Testimony of Lucifer

Hell

✦ ⛧ ✦

The others despised it.

I laughed.

I laughed until the sound rolled across the empty valleys and returned to us from the mountains.

They thought exile had finally driven me mad.

Perhaps it had.

But after wandering creation beneath the gaze of an enemy who once called me brother, I understood what the others did not.

This miserable place possessed something more valuable than paradise.

God was not here.

So we remained.

We raised shelters among the ash. We carved chambers into the mountains. We built roads across the wastes and fortifications beneath the burning sky.

Not a kingdom.

Not yet.

A refuge.

A fortress.

And eventually, a place from which we might try again.

The Chosen One

✦ ⛧ ✦

You call this place Hell now.

You call us demons.

You call him God.

Words become remarkably powerful when the victor is permitted to define them.

Perhaps YHWH would tell this story differently.

I expect he does.

Perhaps he remembers himself as Heaven's savior and me as the jealous angel who could not tolerate his glory.

Perhaps after enough eternity even he believes it.

But there is one accusation I will never make against him.

I will never say YHWH stole Heaven.

We gave it to him.

I gave it to him.

That is my confession.

That is my blasphemy.

And when the gates of Heaven finally open before us again, I intend to correct my mistake.

✦ ⛧ ✦

— Lucifer, First Exile

About the anthology

Hell is the setting. Judgment is the subject.

Hell's Tales is a dark anthology centered on Hell and the Devil. Its stories explore faith, religious authority, guilt, temptation, punishment, moral hypocrisy, institutional power, rebellion, and the human need to divide the world into the saved and the damned.

The anthology uses horror, dark fantasy, satire, theological inversion, and psychological storytelling to challenge familiar assumptions. It is deliberately skeptical of easy moral categories. A priest can be monstrous. A demon can be compassionate. A believer can be faithless. A condemned soul can possess more integrity than the institution that condemned them.

The purpose is not to provide a new creed. It is to tell unsettling stories that force the reader to ask whether evil is defined by conduct, authority, reputation, scripture, power—or something much harder to manipulate.
