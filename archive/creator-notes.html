Creator Notes

Notes on the anthology's use of religious myth and horror.

The stories give physical form to the harm their characters once dismissed. A bishop must hold a dead child; a congregation sees the cost of its decisions beneath the courtroom floor.

The central question

The stories ask who benefits from a verdict and whose testimony the court refused to hear.

Satan as contested character

Satan's account of Heaven comes from an exile who helped create the rule he later opposed.

Why horror?

Horror lets a punishment confront its subject with the people affected by his choices.

Visual direction

The illustrations use scorched parchment and dark engravings, with red and orange light from the furnaces.

Narrative rule

The condemned are permitted complexity. Institutions do not become moral merely by declaring themselves sacred.

Reader's role

Readers hear conflicting witnesses and must decide which accounts they trust.

Intent

The stories put religious claims under questioning, including the claims made by Hell.
